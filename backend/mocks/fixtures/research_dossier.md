OVERVIEW

The internet began as a small US military research project in the late 1960s and grew into the defining infrastructure of modern civilisation. What started as a way to share data between four university computers is now a global network connecting more than five billion people, carrying trillions of dollars of commerce, and mediating virtually every aspect of public and private life.

KEY TIMELINE

1969 — ARPANET goes live, connecting UCLA, Stanford Research Institute, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah. The first message ("LO") crashes the system after two characters.
1972 — Email is invented by Ray Tomlinson, who chooses the @ symbol to separate user from host.
1974 — Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn publish the TCP/IP specification, laying the universal language of the internet.
1983 — ARPANET formally switches to TCP/IP, a date some call the true "birthday" of the internet.
1991 — Tim Berners-Lee launches the World Wide Web at CERN, making the internet accessible to non-specialists.
1993 — The Mosaic browser brings graphics to the web and triggers a surge in public adoption.
1995–2000 — The dot-com boom sees thousands of internet companies flooded with speculative capital.
2000 — The dot-com bubble bursts; hundreds of companies collapse overnight.
2004 — Facebook launches. Social media begins reshaping human communication.
2007 — The iPhone arrives; mobile internet becomes the dominant mode of access within a decade.
2023 — Generative AI integrations begin reshaping how people search, create, and interact online.

MAJOR FIGURES

Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn — Co-inventors of TCP/IP, the foundational protocol suite. Often called "Fathers of the Internet."
Tim Berners-Lee — Invented the World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN, making internet content navigable via hyperlinks.
Marc Andreessen — Co-created Mosaic (1993) and later Netscape, the browser that brought the web to mass audiences.
Ray Tomlinson — Invented email and the use of @ in email addresses.
Larry Page and Sergey Brin — Founded Google in 1998, transforming how people find information online.

CONTROVERSIES

Surveillance — Revelations by Edward Snowden in 2013 exposed mass government surveillance of internet traffic globally.
Misinformation — Social media platforms have been criticised for amplifying false information at unprecedented scale.
Data monopolies — A handful of corporations (Google, Meta, Amazon) control a disproportionate share of internet infrastructure and data.
Net neutrality — Ongoing political battles over whether ISPs can prioritise certain traffic over others.
Digital divide — Billions of people, primarily in the Global South, still lack reliable internet access.
