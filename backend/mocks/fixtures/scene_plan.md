SCENE 2

Narration: For years, the network grew in secret — a tool for scientists and the military. But it had a fatal flaw: dozens of separate networks that could not speak to each other.
Duration: 16 seconds
Purpose: VISUAL: An animated map of disconnected network islands floating in digital space. To show the fragmentation problem that TCP/IP would solve.
Emotion: tension

Narration: In 1974, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published a paper that changed everything. TCP/IP — a universal language for machines. Now any network could talk to any other.
Duration: 16 seconds
Purpose: VISUAL: Two men at a chalkboard surrounded by equations and network diagrams. To introduce the inventors of TCP/IP and their world-changing protocol.
Emotion: intellectual excitement

SCENE 3

Narration: Still, the internet was invisible to ordinary people. That changed in 1989, when a quiet physicist at CERN in Geneva wrote a proposal for linking documents across the network.
Duration: 16 seconds
Purpose: VISUAL: A researcher alone in a dimly lit office, typing at a terminal with a blinking cursor. To set the scene for Tim Berners-Lee's invention of the World Wide Web.
Emotion: anticipation

Narration: Tim Berners-Lee called his idea the World Wide Web. His manager wrote "vague but exciting" in the margins and approved it. Two years later, the web went live.
Duration: 16 seconds
Purpose: VISUAL: The first web browser displaying the original CERN website on a NeXT computer. To mark the moment the internet became navigable for everyone.
Emotion: breakthrough
